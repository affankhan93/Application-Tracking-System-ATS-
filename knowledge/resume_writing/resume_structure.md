# Resume structure

## Recommended section order
Freshers and early-career candidates: Header, Summary, Skills, Projects, Experience or Internships, Education, Certifications.
Experienced candidates: Header, Summary, Experience, Skills, Projects, Education. Put the strongest evidence for the target role first.

## Header
Include name, phone number, a professional email address, city, and links to LinkedIn and GitHub (or a portfolio). Keep it as normal text in the body of the page, not inside a graphic or the page header. A photo, date of birth, marital status and similar personal details are not needed for most roles and can add bias, so leave them out unless the employer asks.

## Summary
Write 2 to 3 lines: the target role, your level, 2 or 3 strongest skills, and one proof point such as a project result. Avoid generic claims like "hardworking team player" that could describe anyone.

## Skills section
Group skills by type, for example Languages, Libraries, Tools, and Cloud. List only skills you can discuss in an interview. Every important skill should also appear as evidence in a project or experience bullet.

## Experience
Use reverse chronological order. For each role give the title, company, location, and dates (month and year), then 3 to 5 bullets. Put the most relevant roles first and keep older or unrelated roles short.

## Projects
Projects are the main proof for freshers. For each of the 2 to 4 best projects give the name, a one-line goal, the tech stack, what you did, the result, and a link to the code or live demo.

## Education
Give the degree, institution, and year. Add marks or CGPA if they are strong. Relevant coursework is optional and mostly useful for freshers.

## Length
One page for freshers and for candidates with up to about five years of experience. Use two pages only when the content clearly earns it.

## Career changes and gaps
Explain a gap or a change of field in one short line, then show what you did in that time: courses, certifications, and projects that prove the new skills.
