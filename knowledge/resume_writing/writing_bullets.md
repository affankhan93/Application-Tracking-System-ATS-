# Writing strong resume bullets

## The basic formula
Start with a strong action verb, say what you did, say how (the tool or method), and end with the result. A good bullet answers: what did you do, with what, and what changed because of it.

## Action verbs by purpose
- Building: built, developed, designed, implemented, engineered, automated, deployed, launched.
- Analysis: analysed, evaluated, modelled, forecasted, identified, validated, tested.
- Improvement: reduced, increased, improved, optimised, streamlined, accelerated, simplified.
- Leadership: led, coordinated, mentored, organised, trained, managed.
- Communication: presented, documented, negotiated, collaborated, reported.

## Weak phrases to replace
"Responsible for", "worked on", "helped with", "involved in", "duties included", "participated in", and "assisted with" describe a task, not an achievement. Replace them with a verb that shows what you personally did and what it produced.

## Quantify honestly
Numbers make a bullet believable. Useful numbers: size (records, users, accounts, budget), speed (time saved, faster delivery), quality (accuracy, error rate, satisfaction), and money (cost saved, revenue added). If you do not have an exact number, use a fair estimate and mark it as approximate. Never invent numbers. If there is no number, describe the scope and the outcome instead.

## Tense and length
Use past tense for finished work and present tense for your current role. Keep each bullet to one or two lines and one idea. Write 3 to 5 bullets per role. Do not use first-person words such as "I" or "my".

## What to avoid
Repeating the same verb at the start of every bullet, long paragraphs, buzzwords without proof, and bullets that only list tools without saying what you built with them.
