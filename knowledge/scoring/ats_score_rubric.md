# ATS score rubric (out of 100)

The ATS score is a guide for improving a resume. It supports a human decision and is never a reason to reject someone automatically. The score uses only the text a person can actually see.

## Components and weights
| Component | Points |
|---|---|
| Parsability and formatting | 20 |
| Keyword and skill match | 30 |
| Section completeness | 15 |
| Experience quality | 25 |
| Readability and length | 10 |

## Parsability and formatting (20)
Measures how cleanly the text can be read. Points are lost for scanned or image-only files, multiple columns, tables, text boxes, missing standard headings, and contact details inside headers or footers. To gain points, use a single column, standard headings, and a text-based PDF or DOCX.

## Keyword and skill match (30)
Measures how well the resume covers the skills and tools the role requires. With a job description, the match is against that description. Without one, it is against the role profile. Required skills count more than nice-to-have skills. A skill earns full credit when it appears in context in a project or experience bullet, and only partial credit when it appears only in a list. Missing required skills are named in the feedback.

## Section completeness (15)
Checks for contact details with links, a summary, skills, experience or projects, and education. Freshers can replace experience with projects and internships. Points are lost for missing sections or a confusing order.

## Experience quality (25)
Measures how strong the bullets are. Points are earned for action verbs, specific tools, numbers and results, relevance to the target role, and recent work. Points are lost for weak phrases such as "responsible for" and "worked on", and for bullets that list tasks without outcomes.

## Readability and length (10)
Rewards one page for most candidates and two at most, bullets of one or two lines, consistent dates and formatting, and few spelling errors. Points are lost for long paragraphs and cluttered layouts.

## Score bands
- 85 to 100: strong. Minor polishing only.
- 70 to 84: good. A few clear fixes would raise it.
- 55 to 69: needs work. Several important gaps.
- Below 55: weak. Needs a rewrite of structure and content.

## How to explain a score
Name the components that lost the most points, give the reason in plain words, and give one concrete fix for each. Do not claim a score is exact. If the integrity scan flagged the file, say that the score was calculated from visible text only and that the flag needs human review.
