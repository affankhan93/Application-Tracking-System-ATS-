# ATS formatting rules

Applicant tracking systems read a resume as text. Layouts that look good to people can confuse a parser, so keep the structure simple.

## Layout
Use a single column. Tables, text boxes, and multiple columns can make a parser read across columns and mix unrelated lines. Do not place key content in the page header or footer, because some parsers skip those areas. Do not use images, icons, or logos to carry text, and avoid skill-bar graphics.

## Section headings
Use standard names: Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, Education, Certifications. Creative headings such as "My Journey" may not be recognised.

## Fonts and sizes
Use common fonts such as Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, or Times New Roman. Body text should be 10 to 12 pt and headings 12 to 14 pt. Do not go below about 9 pt. Keep margins between 0.5 and 1 inch.

## Bullets and symbols
Use simple round or hyphen bullets. Avoid emoji and unusual symbols, which can turn into broken characters after parsing.

## Dates and contact details
Use one date format throughout, for example Mon YYYY to Mon YYYY. Put contact details in the main body. Write links out in full, for example https://github.com/username, instead of only a clickable word.

## File type
Submit a text-based PDF or a DOCX file. A scanned or image-only PDF has no text layer, so the system must guess the words with OCR, which makes mistakes. Export from Word or Google Docs rather than printing to an image. Follow the job posting if it asks for a specific format. Keep the file small, under about 2 MB.

## Colour
Use dark text on a white background. White text on a dark banner is fine when the contrast is strong. Never use text that matches or nearly matches its background colour.

## Quick test
Open the PDF, select all the text, and paste it into a plain text editor. If the order is scrambled, words are missing, or characters look wrong, the layout needs fixing.
